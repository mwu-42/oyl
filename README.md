# Oyl

**Own your life.** That's what Oyl stands for: a macOS notch app, and Oyl, the little imp who lives in it, keeping an eye on everything going on for you (your agents at work, your music, your timers, your Mac) so you don't have to.

Hover the notch for music (with a visualizer Oyl surfs across as the song plays, which you can drag Oyl along to scrub, lyrics that stay on from song to song until you turn them off (synced lyrics from LRCLIB, a free database people add songs to, so one just out may not have them for a while: a song it hasn't is asked after again an hour on, a lookup that got no answer the next time the song plays, and a title with a featured artist or a version on it, "(feat. …)" or " - Remastered", is looked for without that too; in Midnight they float in the sky under the open notch, whatever page is up, the sung line's words lighting like stars as the last line dissolves into stardust; the phone remote shows them too; and in Midnight a setting keeps them up while the notch is closed, right in the menu bar's strip beside it, on no ground of their own: each line fades in faintly, then sparkles in word by word on the other side of the notch from the last (a break between them aside, so they're sung either side about as much), the first on the left, as the last fades slowly away, a long one stretching most of the way along its side before it shrinks; a break in a song, and a song without lyrics all through, is a constellation either side of the notch, flaring and rippling to the beat; from song to song, and as the music pauses and plays, they fade quickly out and in; and never in the way of a click), cover-art controls (always the song's own cover: Spotify shows a song switched to its music video with a still from the video, so Oyl looks up the song's cover instead, the video playing on in Spotify), and the song's name, which a click takes you to Spotify or Music from, with everyone it's by under it (Spotify's scripting tells only a song's first artist, so Oyl reads them all off the song's embed page on Spotify's site as it starts, sending it only the song's ID; they ease in a moment after, and the music Oyl's row and its hover say them too); a song Spotify's playing on another device, like your phone, is followed as the Mac's Spotify tells of each change (a song starting, a pause, a skip, a seek), so its lyrics keep time, though its scripting can go on saying an old song's playing here; with nothing told yet, a song that isn't heard on the Mac counts as paused), timers and reminders (a name can be as long as you like, wrapping onto as many lines as it takes, and ⌘C, ⌘V, ⌘X, ⌘A and ⌘Z work as they do anywhere; click one to change its time or name, or + to type how much more it gets, which lengthens what it was set for as well as what's left; one that's gone off can have one or five more; pin one to hold it on the closed notch, its ring and name left of the notch (an unnamed one, just its ring) and its time right, in either look, and from the phone too; or type “/pin” anywhere on its line, as you start it (“25m essay /pin”) or change it, or in its name on the phone), an Amphetamine-style keep-awake (type “+30m” for more of a set time; with the lid closed and no other screen on, once it's stayed down two seconds, it turns the screen and the keyboard's light all the way down and off, putting them back as they were last set while you were using the Mac, not as auto-brightness dims them as the lid comes down, mutes the Mac's own speakers (not headphones; Mute speakers when shut) and, if you like, puts on Low Power Mode (Low Power Mode when shut, which asks for your password once; on battery, then and below 40% or while it's running hot), and Oyl stops drawing, analyzing the music and polling the player till the lid opens, the screen woken as it does; it stops by itself on battery at 20% or if the battery can't be read twice running, and always if the Mac gets critically hot, saying why; its hold on idle sleep lets go by itself at a set time's end, even if Oyl's hung; and if Oyl crashes or is force-quit, a watchdog it starts alongside turns lid sleep back on (and Low Power Mode off) a minute later unless it's back, as it does if Oyl's hung over two minutes past a set time's end. Much of it after Insomnia's way of doing it), the Oyls page (where timers that went off in the past day rest, a click from running again), an activity monitor that also counts Claude Code tokens, and what they'd cost at Anthropic's API prices (hour by hour, today and in the last five hours: each reply priced by its model, cache reads and writes, fast mode and web searches included, from the pricing page as of November 2026; a subscription doesn't charge by the token, so it's what the same use would cost on the API), and under them your GitHub contributions over the past year, the grid on your profile (a square a day, a week to a column, brighter the more you did; commits, pull requests, issues and reviews as GitHub counts them, private ones too), read with GitHub's own gh tool as it's signed in (so Oyl keeps no sign-in of its own; without it, or signed out, the grid says how to set it up), when the page opens and every ten minutes while it's open, the last one kept so it's there at once. Beside it, the year's total; point at a day for its count and date, click it to see what you did that day on GitHub. In Oyl's look it's GitHub's grid in the page's pink, today ringed; in Midnight Oyl's a star chart, each day a star from a mote of dust to a four-pointed star for the busiest, the bright ones twinkling and a run of them in a week joined into a constellation, today's circled, the one pointed at swelling in moonlight. GitHub grid in the settings turns it off. Then a mirror (the camera, for a look at yourself before a call; its tab can be turned off on the Oyls tab). Out on the menu bar, an Oyl turns up for each thing going on (and when it's over, waves goodbye where it stands, springs up twice and is gone in a puff of smoke, sparks of its color flying out of it; one whose work is done jumps for joy first; in Midnight a moyl bows, bobs twice and flares, scattering into stardust. No two ever walk along together looking like one: one sets off a moment after another, a little quicker or slower, or somewhere else): each Claude Code agent at work (in a pastel with an orange hat; in a terminal, an editor or the Claude app's Code tab), each of the Claude app's Cowork tasks while it works and for a quarter of an hour after (read off the transcript each keeps on the Mac, whether or not it's the one on screen, in any of the app's accounts), the chat showing in the Claude app, Muse (Meta's agent, open at muse.ai in Chrome or Safari, in a black hat: working while its page offers to stop and what it says is changing (Muse leaves its Stop up once it's done, so a page that's sat still a minute is done), and wanting you while it asks you to approve something, then gone once it's answered; a page that's offered to stop for over five minutes is at a task of its own in the background, so doesn't count; read while its tab's the one showing, which needs Oyl allowed to control the browser and in Accessibility; click it to bring its tab up), every other agent on the radar (below), an app taking notes (Granola in a meeting: a sage Oyl with a notepad, scribbling, now and then looking up at what's being said, for as long as it has the microphone, which macOS tells of any app without asking; in Midnight, a banded world ruled like paper, a little moon going busily round it for the pen), the song (in headphones, dragging its cover and a little visualizer along behind it, bopping its head to the beat, which Oyl finds in the music as it plays, and singing along once a pinky's shown the lyrics; for an audiobook, sat reading it instead, looking up now and then to think it over, its cover in a thought cloud), every timer and reminder (its countdown beside it, or with Countdowns off on the Oyls tab, only in its last three minutes), staying awake, a low battery, a late night, a fresh selfie. Right of the notch sits a calendar bar, with today's events along it (the Oyls tab can turn it off). At the top left of the menu bar is the name of the desktop you're on: “desktop 2”, as Mission Control numbers them, or, in a fullscreen app's Space, the app's name, in lower case, till you call it something else (kept just as you type it, capitals and all). It's up for 3 seconds each time you go to a desktop (and as Oyl or it is turned on), then fades: longer while the pointer's on it, or while you're renaming it, and for 3 seconds more once you have. Click it while it's up to rename it (Return keeps what's typed, Escape doesn't, clicking anywhere else keeps it too; nothing typed puts its own name back); a fullscreen app's name stays with its window, even as it leaves fullscreen and comes back, till the Mac restarts. In Oyl's look it's a piece of the notch in black, flush with the screen's top and left, its corner rounded and its top flared into the edge, a new desktop's name fading in over the last; in Midnight's, a patch of night with no edge, a glint twinkling before the name that flares as you go to another desktop, the name coming in out of a blur. On a desktop it sits over the Apple menu (and the app's, as far as it reaches), so a right-click on it opens whichever of them is under it (that needs Accessibility); in a fullscreen app it's in the black strip by the notch, and the menu bar slides down over it as ever, a click on it then going to the menu bar. The Oyls keep off it, and it's away while Oyl's off or resting, in Mission Control and under Midnight's band; Desktop's name on the Oyls tab turns it off.

Picture in picture works for any window, and over every desktop and every fullscreen app (a browser's own doesn't come over another app's fullscreen Space): ⌃⌥P puts the window in front into it, small (the one the app has its focus in, as Accessibility says; never the strip a fullscreen app keeps its toolbar in, as Chrome does), and again puts it away (or puts the new window in front in its place); or right-click an open app in Oyl's Dock for Picture in Picture (a menu of its windows, if it has a few; Close Picture in Picture, if one of them's in it). One at a time. It's a live copy of the window, wherever that is: on another desktop, in its own fullscreen Space, under other windows. It's drawn by ScreenCaptureKit, so Oyl has to be allowed Screen Recording (till it is, it says so, and a click on it opens the setting), only at as many pixels as it's shown at (more as you zoom in), thirty frames a second, only while it's up; macOS's screen-recording mark shows in the menu bar meanwhile. It takes the window's shape from what ScreenCaptureKit sends, and shows just the window in it, wherever in the frame it's put. Drag it anywhere; drag a corner to make it bigger or smaller, or an edge to change its shape (the window then fills it, what's over cropped off). Pinch, or ⌘-scroll, to zoom in on part of the window (a video on a page, say), scroll to move about it, a two-finger double tap zooms in or back out, and Fit puts it back as it was. Double-click it, or its arrow, to go to the window (its app comes forward and macOS goes to the Space it's on); × puts it away. The next one comes up where and as big as the last was (low on the right, the first time), and it goes once the window's closed or its app quits, Oyl's switched off, or the feature is; it's out of the way in Mission Control. In Oyl's look it's the window with rounded corners and a shadow, × and the arrow coming up either end of a shade along its top, the window's name between, on a plain ease, as the pointer comes over it. In Midnight Oyl's it has no edge: the window fades out into the desktop all round, a glint twinkling at its top right and a fainter one low on its left, motes of starlight drifting round in the fading edge; × is traced in silver at its top left and the arrow at its bottom right, the app's name in small capitals along the bottom, and it comes in out of a blur and goes back into one. ⌃⌥P Picture in picture on the Keys tab turns it off.

Oyl watches for agents universally, on any Mac, with its agent radar: a list of the agents it knows and how each gives itself away, since there's no one sign on a Mac that an AI is at work. Claude Code by its hooks (or, where they aren't set up, as a program); the Claude app's chats by its window (even from another Space: in a fullscreen one of its own, its window's still read, and its prompts to use a tool still come up for your OK; and a task waiting on you that isn't the one showing, as its sidebar says ("Awaiting …", "Needs …"), gets a card too, whose Allow or Deny opens the task and answers its prompt once it's up, or leaves it open in front if what it wants is something else), and Cowork by the transcripts its tasks keep. Agents and chats in a browser tab by their pages, which nearly all offer to stop while they work and ask you to approve or allow what they want to do: Muse, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, Grok, Copilot, DeepSeek, Le Chat, Manus, Lovable, v0, Bolt and Replit. Apps by their windows, the same way: the ChatGPT app, Codex and Perplexity. Programs run in a terminal or an editor by how busy they are (one at its prompt uses next to nothing; one at work, streaming its reply, a steady few percent of a core), each its own Oyl named for the folder it's working in, while it's at work and a while after: Codex, Gemini CLI, Aider, OpenCode, Goose, Amp, Cursor Agent, Crush, Qwen Code and Copilot CLI. And note takers by the microphone: Granola. Each agent's Oyl wears its own color of hat while it works (Claude's orange, ChatGPT's and Codex's green, Gemini's blue, Muse's black…). For anything else, bring up the app (or the site, in a browser) and right-click the notch: Watch it as an Agent, and it's on the list, watched by its windows (or its page) from then on; Stop Watching takes it off. And Oyl looks for agents it doesn't know: every half minute, which apps and programs hold a connection open to an AI service (OpenAI's, Anthropic's, Mistral's, Groq's, OpenRouter's, Deepgram's and the like), and one that keeps doing so is offered, once, at the foot of the Oyls page: "Notion seems to use AI. Watch it as an agent?" (No, and it's never asked again.) Browsers aren't, since a browser is one app for all its tabs, nor editors built on VS Code, whose windows can't be read without switching on their screen-reader support. All of it is read on the Mac, from what macOS tells any app of its own user's processes; nothing is sent anywhere.

Every feature can be turned off. The settings tab, the gear last in the notch's row of tabs (or Settings… on the notch's right-click), is a page of them all, in sections: the notch's pages (each tab can go), lyrics, the visualizer (and with it, listening to the music), the song's name opening its player, haptics, Wispr Flow; each kind of Oyl (agents', the music's, timers', keep-awake's, the battery's, bedtime's, the selfie's, the one hanging out), the countdowns, dimming the bar, the calendar bar; each agent, the radar's and yours (yours with an × to take them off the list), answering Claude Code in the notch, and looking for new ones; hand gestures, and each gesture on its own; the control taps, the song keys and keeping out of Mission Control's way; the Dock, each shelf, and labeling screenshots; Midnight Oyl, and opening at login. Point at one to see what it does. In Midnight Oyl the page is engraved, its rows drifting in one after another; under them all, the motto: Oyl, own your life.

The shelf, Recent, in Oyl's Dock (below), keeps what's new in one row, newest first, each tile marked with a dot for where it came from: your latest screenshots and screen recordings as you take them, wherever screenshots are set to go (light green); whatever you've just saved or downloaded (blue, below); and the files Claude Code's just written or edited (orange: Oyl hears of each Write, Edit and notebook edit through its hooks once it's done, not Claude's own files in ~/.claude, what it only scribbles in a temporary folder, or a repository's .git; the latest eight still there, remembered across launches, a file it made in Downloads showing as Claude's). The card's top says what each color means. Each of the three can be turned off on the Oyls tab, and the shelf itself. Its button (a tray) sits first past the Dock's line, what's on it only on the shelf: click it and the shelf comes up over the bar, to drag them straight into a chat, an email or any other app; the pictures are a little bigger than they were. Each is labeled with what's in it, on the Mac (Vision reads its text, and Apple's on-device model puts that in a few words where Apple Intelligence is on, else the biggest line is used; nothing leaves the Mac), the label on its tile over when it was taken, and the file renamed to it as soon as it has one (if it still has the name macOS gave it), so it goes into a chat named for what it is; right-click one from before to rename it too. The Dock fades out of the way once one's dragged off it, so the app under it can take the drop, or drop it on an app in the Dock to open it there. Drag any file onto the shelf's button or the shelf itself (or use its +) to keep it there too; right-click the button to choose files, or to open Downloads or the screenshots' folder. Hovering a tile shows two ways to be rid of it, apart: × top right takes it off the shelf (the file stays, and comes back if it's saved again), the bin top left moves the file to the Trash. And low right, AirDrop (straight to its picker) and a way to convert it to another format, saved beside the original: pictures to PNG, JPEG, HEIC, TIFF, GIF, BMP or PDF; a PDF's pages to pictures or its text to Word, RTF, HTML or plain text; Markdown, Word, RTF, HTML and plain text to PDF and each other; movies to MP4, MOV, an animated GIF (the first 30 seconds) or just their sound; sound to M4A, WAV, AIFF or FLAC; JSON, CSV and property lists to each other; folders to a zip and zips back. Anything at all can be zipped. Right-click a tile to copy, open, AirDrop or show it, take it off the shelf (the file stays) or move it to the Trash. Click a tile to select it, shift-click another to select everything between, or ⌘-click to add one (or drop one): they drag out together (into a chat, a folder, or onto an app in the Dock), and the buttons on a selected one, and its right-click menu, do the same to them all (converting to what they all convert to). The card's top says how many are selected; Clear, a click off the tiles or putting the shelf away lets them go. ⌘-drag a tile out to use it once: it goes wherever it's dropped (a chat, an email, a folder, an app in the Dock), and then into the Trash, with the sound macOS makes when something goes in it. Its picture carries a bin mark as it goes (a dark disc in Oyl's look, ink ringed in silver in Midnight Oyl's, as its own bin button is), so it's plain it won't be back. What goes out is a copy Oyl sets aside in its caches (a clone on the same disk, taking no room), so the app it went to can still read it once the original's gone: a browser uploading it, the Finder copying it. The copy goes a day later. Let go nowhere (or back on the shelf), it all stays as it was. ⌘-drag a selected one and they all go; one from Photos is only taken off the shelf, as its bin does. ⌘-clicking still adds one to the selection, once you let go without dragging. Selected, a tile's ringed in white with its name on a pill in Oyl's look; in Midnight Oyl's, moonlight comes up inside it, breathing, and a star twinkles by its name. What's been saved is (up to twelve, as with screenshots): what's in Downloads (what comes by AirDrop, downloads from the web, attachments saved out of a text with Save Attachment, and what's saved since into a folder in it, two folders down, as an app exporting into a folder of its own there; a folder that comes in whole, unzipped say, is one tile, not each file in it; and a file saved over an old one, with Replace or by saving it again, which macOS dates as the old one but for when it was changed, as of when it's saved), what's come from elsewhere onto the Desktop (an attachment dragged out of Messages, say), and photos and videos saved into Photos from Messages (Add to Photos Library, Save Photo) or another app, on the Mac or an iPhone, but not what a camera took or your own screenshots. Not what's still downloading, or the Mac's screenshots, which are on as screenshots. A photo's copied out of Photos into Oyl's own folder, so it drags and converts like any file (taking it off the shelf leaves it in Photos). The first time it's opened macOS asks to let Oyl see Downloads and Photos. The shelf grows out of its tile in the bar as it opens, on the notch's spring, and goes back into it; in Midnight Oyl, the Dock's sky grows on up round it instead, new stars drawing on in the new room, and the shelf comes in out of a blur on a pool of night with no edge to it. The first time the shelf's opened, macOS asks to let Oyl see the Desktop.

Oyl has its own Dock too, along the bottom of the screen in the notch's look (the open notch turned over, in black; in Midnight Oyl, the band turned over, the screen's whole width: it blooms up and out of the foot of the screen from where the pointer brought it up, each icon coming on out of a blur as the bloom reaches it, quicker than the band does, as the band does from the notch, and wilts back into it as it goes, its edge flowing in slow swells and lobes and dissolving into the desktop, the star chart's hour lines fanning out from the middle, stars glinting, star dust drifting and filaments and drops of light spilling up out of it). It shows what the macOS Dock does, read from its settings at its icon size: Finder, the apps kept in it, any others open, its folders (but not the Trash). Each is redrawn in the look: its glyph lifted off its icon (what stands out from the tile's own colors), white on a pale tile in Oyl's, as Oyl's own buttons are, and in Midnight Oyl's traced in silver with no tile, glints twinkling along its lines that brighten and quicken when it's pointed at. Finder's is a magnifying glass, Outlook's just the outline of the open envelope on its icon (no O), Chrome's Google's G, Canva's the C of its name, and Activity Monitor's just its pulse, without the grid behind it. An icon with no one glyph on it stays as it is. An app with no icon of its own (shown by macOS with the plain one apps get) is a four-pointed star's outline with the first letter of its name in it, drawn the same way in either look. Like the notch, it comes up as the pointer reaches the bottom edge anywhere under where its icons would be (end to end, as wide as the bar is with what's in it now, on that screen; the corners and the edge either side of the bar are left alone) and stays there just a moment (0.15 s, so passing over it doesn't bring it up); in Oyl's look its icons pop up in a quick wave out from the pointer, the nearest first, covering the macOS Dock (which should be set to hide itself; while Oyl's Dock is on, Oyl keeps it down, so it never comes up at the bottom edge at all: it tells it to wait for ever first, its autohide-delay, and puts your own setting back once Oyl's Dock is off, Oyl's switched off or it's quit; Keep the macOS Dock down on the Oyls tab turns that off), and goes when the pointer leaves, though not at a slip: it has some room round it, and waits a moment before it goes, more of both with a shelf up (beside the bar under the shelf counts too), and a little longer just after a menu's closed somewhere off it. Click an app to open or switch to it, right-click to hide it, quit it or show it in Finder. ⌘-click one that's open for another window of it, as its own New Window makes one (an app without one gets a new instance of it instead); ⌥-click one to quit it, once you've said so over its icon (Quit, or Cancel; or click elsewhere, or move away). Finder can't be quit, so ⌥-clicking it just opens it. Drag one along the Dock to move it, the others making way (Finder stays first); the order is Oyl's own, and the macOS Dock's is left as it is. The shelf is in it (above). An app asking for you (a message come in, a download done: what has the macOS Dock bounce its icon) doesn't bounce while Oyl's Dock is on: instead a small mark comes up out of the bottom edge, under where its icon is in the bar, and stays there till you go to the app (or it quits), even if it only asked for a moment, as most do; in Oyl's look a little of the Dock, black, flared into the edge, the app's icon on it, and in Midnight Oyl's its glyph traced in starlight just over the edge on a pool of night, breathing, a glint twinkling by it. Its icon still pops up, as the Dock's would, but less: two low hops as it starts asking (a third of the Dock's bounce, the second lower), then one lower hop a minute while it's still asking, and the same from its icon in the bar while that's up; in Oyl's look up and down on a plain ease, in Midnight Oyl's lifted slowly as if on the air, moonlight flaring round it. With the Dock up, the app's icon has a soft red dot high on its right (a glint in Midnight Oyl). Oyl hears of it as the Dock does, from LaunchServices, looking twice a second, at only the apps in the bar that are open (a fraction of a millisecond; one that asks only for the Dock's one bounce does for about a second, so none is missed), and keeps the macOS Dock's icons from bouncing meanwhile (its no-bouncing setting, which it reads each time, so it's never restarted for it), putting yours back however Oyl quits; Apps asking for you on the Oyls tab turns it off. It comes up mid-drag too, as the Dock does: drop files on an app to open them in it. But not while you're selecting: dragging down a document to select (and scroll) as you go, or a box round files, or moving a window, it stays down; only a drag with something to drop (files, a link, a picture) brings it up. To turn the Dock off, untick Oyl's Dock on the Oyls tab (or right-click the notch). Right-click the notch to quit Oyl (it opens again at login, unless that's turned off in Settings), or Turn Off Oyl, which also keeps it from opening at login till you open it again; the Oyls tab has Turn off Oyl too. Either ends a keep-awake. Tap Command twice on its own (either one), quickly (the second within a quarter of a second of letting go of the first, so pressing it twice on the way to a shortcut isn't one), to take a screenshot of part of the screen, just as ⇧⌘4 does: Oyl presses the Mac's own shortcut for it, whatever it's set to, so it's saved and shown as macOS's are; with that shortcut turned off, `screencapture` takes it, which needs Oyl allowed Screen Recording. Tap control three times to switch Oyl off without quitting: the notch blinks and everything's put away, the camera and microphone with it, till three more taps bring it back (or Oyl starts again). Switching off ends a keep-awake too (and the two-tap screenshot with it), and meanwhile Claude Code asks for its OKs the usual way; timers carry on, and still chime. The taps need Oyl allowed in Accessibility, as the hand pointer does. Control-Option-→ skips to the next song from any app, and Control-Option-← goes back (to the start of the song, or the one before if it's only just begun, as ⏮ does); nothing on the Mac uses them (VS Code only, to move by part of a word), and they're only taken while there's a song on and Oyl's on, so otherwise they go through as ever. They need Accessibility too. Command-period brings up the emoji picker from any app, as the Windows key and period do on Windows: Oyl presses the Mac's own shortcut for it (Control-Command-Space, Emoji & Symbols in any app's Edit menu), so it comes up at the caret wherever you're typing, the notch's own fields too. It's the picker in every app, terminals and code editors too, in place of their own Command-period (Cancel in a dialog, which Escape still is; Stop in a terminal, which Control-C still is; Quick Fix in VS Code and the editors built on it, which can be given another key in its Keyboard Shortcuts). It needs Accessibility too (but not in the notch), and ⌘. Emoji on the Oyls tab turns it off. While you're viewing your desktops (Mission Control, App Exposé or Show Desktop), the notch, the Oyls and the Dock keep out of the way, none of them opening or taking a click till it's gone; that needs Accessibility too.

Control and Option tapped together turn the camera on quietly for 3 seconds, wherever the pointer is and whether the notch is open or not, so a gesture works from anywhere (nothing shows it but the Mac's camera light); tapped twice, it stays on till they're tapped twice again, or an OK's held up (with gestures off, they do nothing). With the camera on, hand gestures over the open notch play, pause and skip, turn the Mac's volume (pinch and twist; add the pinky to scrub), take a selfie (✌️; it's saved in ~/Pictures/Oyl side by side with a screenshot of your screen, once Oyl's allowed Screen Recording, though its Oyl holds up just you), keep the Mac awake (🤟), and show the lyrics (pinky out, thumb tucked in). Holding the ring finger down with the thumb, the other three fingers up, makes every Oyl say what it's doing; tipped over to the left, that hand sends them dashing left to line up, and to the right, flings them off to the right. The camera tracks at 640×480 (full size only for a selfie) and reads fewer frames when the Mac is busy or hot. Center Stage is off for Oyl alone (other apps keep it as you have it), so the camera sees all it can and holds still: where your hand is in the picture always means the same place.

With [Wispr Flow](https://wisprflow.ai) installed, the notch shows when it's listening, however you started it: a microphone on one side of the closed notch and your voice on the other (with microphone access, which Oyl only uses then). The microphone button at the top right of the open notch starts and stops its hands-free dictation, through links Wispr Flow handles but doesn't document, so a later version could break it.

The ideas page (the lightbulb tab) is a place to dump ideas to come back to, kept in tod's brain dump so they're in tod too. Type one and press Return; the field stays ready for the next. Or jot one about something on screen: click the camera at the end of the field, and the notch gets out of the way (Oyl's Dock stays down too) while you pick out an area as ⇧⌘4 has you (Space for a window, Escape to call it off); then it's back, the screenshot at the head of the field and the field ready for what the idea is. Return keeps it with the idea, a small picture by its title: click it to open it, or drag it into a chat. Point at the one in the field for an × to let it go. The screenshots are kept in Oyl's own folder, named for each idea (tod keeps only the idea), and go to the Trash a day after their idea's gone from tod (not just checked off). The first time, macOS may ask to let Oyl record the screen. Each idea is one of three kinds, read from the same line. A deadline (“essay due fri”, or just a day, “lab report thu 5pm”) can't slip: it's always in sight under Due, soonest first, and says when it's late. An aim (“plan the trip in a month or so”, “by next week”, “early dec”, “by fri”, “dentist next week-ish”) is a day to have it done by, roughly, and can slip: it waits under Later, said as loosely as it was meant (“mid Dec”), moves up to Coming up in its last week, and once it's past, says how long it's slipped. Anything else is for Someday, and a month after it was jotted (or last kept) it asks “still want it?”. Point at an idea to put it off a week or a month, or keep it another month; check it off when it's done. Deadlines and aims are kept apart so the aims don't make the deadlines easy to ignore (as Amazing Marvin's due and end dates do, or Todoist's deadlines and dates), aims come back when their time comes as a tickler file's do, and the undated are looked over monthly, as a bullet journal's are. The phone remote has it too, to jot ideas on the go. An idea typed with one of your projects' #names ("#oyl what if…") goes into that project instead, and pointed at, any idea can be made a project of its own (Project: it's checked off in tod, its screenshot going with it).

The projects page (the indented-list tab) keeps track of everything you've got going, bigger than a to-do: areas (ongoing, like a class), projects (with an end), and under them subprojects, assignments and tasks (with subtasks), ideas, notes and screenshots, as deep as they go. It's Oyl's own, kept in its folder (tod's left as it is). Type into its field and it's filed as you type, as Things' and Akiflow's quick entries do: "#oyl fix the dock fri 5pm" is a task in the Oyl project (a project's #name is its name's letters and digits run together, matched as the to-do field matches tod's lists; Tab finishes one, and a #name no project has yet makes a new one), due Friday at 5; "?" first, or the bulb at the field's start lit, makes it an idea instead, its words kept as typed. The camera adds a screenshot to it, as on the ideas page. Without a #project it waits in Unsorted, to be filed (as Linear's triage has it). Under the field are what's pressing (every task with a day within the week, or late, soonest first, each with its project) and what's unsorted; point at one to file it into a project, make an idea a project, or open it in the outline. There are no priorities: what's pressing is what's due. A task with a time reminds you then: the notch pops open with it (in either look), a chime, and +10 min, OK or Done; a reminder missed while Oyl was off goes off when it's back, if it's within 12 hours. The button at the field's end (or Projects… on the notch's right-click) opens the outline, in a window of its own, where you are (over a fullscreen app too): on the left Pressing (every dated task across your projects, late, today, tomorrow, the week, later), Unsorted, and your areas and projects, subprojects under them, then those put off to Someday or done; on the right the one that's open, its page: where it is, its name, its notes, its lines, and the last few check-ins from the work log under its #name. Its lines are typed as in any outliner (Workflowy's, OmniFocus's): Return starts the next (an empty one comes out a level instead), Tab and Shift-Tab indent and outdent, ↑ and ↓ go between lines, ⌘⇧↑ and ⌘⇧↓ move one, ⌘↩ checks a task off, and Delete on an empty line takes it away; ⌘N is a new project, ⌘[ goes up a level. Start a line "? " for an idea, "- " for a note, "# " for a subproject or "[] " for a task; a task's day and time ("essay fri 5pm") are read out of it as it's finished. Each line's ⋯ (or a right-click) has what kind it is, Remind Me (in an hour, this evening, tomorrow morning), Move To another project or Unsorted, a screenshot (taken with the window out of the way, as ⇧⌘4 has you), and Delete (a project, or a line with lines under it, asks first). Pictures dropped on a page come in as lines. A project can be an area, put off to Someday (out of Pressing) or done; done lines go after ten minutes unless Show done is on. In Midnight Oyl the window's the night: stars wheeling slowly round a pole off to the upper right, rings engraved round it, the odd shooting star, the lines drifting in, every word in its Victorian faces, a project's mark a four-pointed star and a task's an engraved ring that becomes one once it's done. Projects page in Settings turns it off.

The work log (the book tab) keeps a record of your work in your own words, for the big picture of it later, or a résumé, a review or an interview. Check in on the page whenever you like (it doesn't ask; its field asks what you got done, a different way each day); the day turns at four in the morning, so a check-in after midnight is the day before's, and "/yesterday" in one makes it the day before's anyway. Type it as you'd say it, starting each project's part with its #tag: "#oyl shipped the dock delay. #cs229 finished pset 3". Each sentence (or line) goes to the tags it starts with or has in it, and one without a tag goes with the one before it (or, at the very start, the first tagged one); "#tag:" starts a new part anywhere, a message with no tag at all goes under General, and C#, #3 and a link's # aren't tags. The #s come out of your words: a tag a sentence starts or ends with goes (unless it's what the sentence is about, after "for", "in" and the like), one in the middle stays as its word. Return files it, ⌥↩ starts a new line and Tab finishes a tag you've started. Under the field are your projects' tags, the latest worked on first, with how many days each has had (click one to start its part, or right-click to open its tab of the log); what tod has had done today, to put in with a click; and what's been filed today, each with an × to take it back out (a project with nothing left goes too). Every project is a tab of a document, Work Log in Documents, in Oyl Work Log (or in Oyl's own folder, if Documents can't be written), which Open the log opens in your browser: an overview of every project, then a tab for each #tag (the story so far, then month by month, each month's story over its days, and under it, each day in your exact words), and a tab of every day; Copy on a tab copies it as text, and printed it's every tab one after another, black on white. It's in Oyl's look, or Midnight Oyl's (engraved, in Victorian type, off to one side, each tab drifting in under a turning sky with the odd shooting star), and there's a Markdown copy beside it. The stories are written on the Mac by Apple's on-device model where Apple Intelligence is on, only from what you wrote (nothing leaves the Mac): a month of a project's days told as one paragraph, and from its months the story so far, each written again only when what it's from has changed, the latest worked-on project first; a month too long to go in at once is told in goes, each carrying on from the last. What you write is kept in Oyl's own folder, the document made again from it as it changes. The phone remote has the check-in too. Work log page is in Settings, under the notch.

Oyl's phone remote puts the notch on your phone, from its browser, over the same Wi-Fi, or from anywhere with [Tailscale](https://tailscale.com) running on both. Turn it on in Settings, under Oyl, then choose Pair a phone: the notch shows a QR code for its camera (or six digits to type at the address beside it), good for five minutes and five tries. Paired, the phone keeps a long random key as a cookie (Oyl keeps only its hash), and the page shows what's waiting for you, with Allow and Deny for Claude Code's requests (while it's waiting on Oyl, about 25 seconds); your agents and what each is doing; the song, with its cover and controls; timers, to start, pause and stop; and keeping the Mac awake. It's in whichever look Oyl is in: Oyl's own, black and rounded like the notch, or Midnight Oyl's, its sky turning slowly round a pole off to one side, engraved rules in place of boxes, each part drifting in. Add it to the Home Screen and it opens like an app. It only answers devices on the Mac's own Wi-Fi or Ethernet (not a VPN, or the internet), sent to the Mac by name, and anything that changes something has to come from its own page. Forget phones, beside Pair, unpairs them all; turned off, nothing's listening. It's plain HTTP, so on its own it's for your own Wi-Fi rather than a shared one. Campus and café networks often keep devices from reaching each other at all. For those, run Tailscale on the Mac and the phone (signed in to the same account). Its tunnel is encrypted and works over any network, a phone's own data too. While it's running, the pairing code and the address go by the Mac's name on your tailnet, so one pairing works at school and at home alike. The remote also answers your devices on the tailnet, but only ones that come in on the Mac's own tailnet address, never a device on the Wi-Fi with an address that looks like one.

Midnight Oyl (right-click the notch, or on the Oyls tab or the settings tab, where its switch is unlike the other toggles, since it changes the whole look: a little sky with the moon at its end, its name set in Midnight's own face; pointed at, its stars come out and the moon comes up, or in Midnight they quicken and the moon glints; clicked, the knob slides over and the look changes behind it) is a second look, made more to be looked at than used: a Victorian celestial atlas plate seen through TouchDesigner's glass. Opening the notch (gently, easing to rest with no bounce) runs a line of night out along the menu bar, then lets it fall into a band across the whole top of the screen, the sky engraved on it in silver-white: a star chart round the notch as its pole, stars that light up outward as it opens, a planisphere and an orrery that draw on and turn, a moon that tells the time (new at midnight, it waxes through the morning, lit by as much of its face as the morning's gone, till it's full at noon; then it wanes, dark by as much as the afternoon and evening have, and round it a dial of the day, midnight at its top, with a glint for now), constellations, drifting star dust and the odd shooting star. Closing runs it all back, nearly as slowly (0.9 s) and on the same curve: the page goes first, blurring and lifting away as it came in, then the band folds back up into the notch, its foot drawing in, its petals parting and its stars going out inward, the planisphere's hours unwinding; it fades only at the last, nearly back in the notch. It has no edge: its foot flows in slow swells and lobes and dissolves into the desktop, with motes, stars, filaments and drops of light spilling out past it. It reaches down only as far as the page needs, a little sky under it. Every word on the pages is set in a High Victorian face, and their colors are paled toward white. With gestures on, the camera's on while it's open but never shown (with them off, it stays off, and the rock sign isn't looked for): your hands are, as constellations in its sky, each joint a star, named for the gesture they're making, so there's no separate camera tab. Click the moon to keep it open as a display, and again to let it close. It stays open only while the pointer's over its page (as big as the open notch would be, and a little round it) or on the way over to the moon, not anywhere in its sky, so it's as easy to leave as the notch. The Oyls are moyls: small, real-looking planets in their Oyls' colors, lit from one side with a thin rim of air, each a world that fits its job (an agent's a banded gas giant with a storm and, while Claude works, an orange polar cap; the song's a ringed one, its rings swelling on the beat (an audiobook's, its rings still, a little moon going slowly round as its glow comes and goes); a timer's an ice giant, its moon going round once over its time; staying awake's an ocean world under cloud; the battery's and bedtime's cratered rock), with a flare for a cheer. They're never on the menu bar: when the band opens they swoop down into its sky and drift among the stars, there to be clicked, and are gone again as it closes; when they announce what they're doing, each wears a ring of engraved capitals all the way round it saying so. On the music page, the wave is a galaxy in the cover's colors, its stars twinkling where the wave rises over them, and riding its crest as the scrubber is a bright four-pointed star. Holding up the rock 'n' roll sign (thumb and little finger out) turns your hands into the pointer, manus mode: each hand is a pointer of its own, a small constellation of it at its index fingertip (its stars born as it starts, out from the wrist, each a faint cloud gathering in and lit in a flash; and as it stops, each flaring and bursting, faintly, in a few sparks), and the cursor (hidden meanwhile, until the trackpad moves it) goes with whichever hand is doing something. Pinching your index finger and thumb clicks (holding the pinch holds, to drag; two quick pinches double-click), and so does resting the pointer on something a moment, a ring of dots drawing round it till it clicks. Touching your thumb to your middle fingertip instead, the index still pointing, right-clicks. Two fingers held together, side by side, are two on the trackpad: move them to scroll. Three together swipe as the trackpad's set to: across between Spaces and full-screen apps, up for Mission Control, down for the app's windows. A flick of the wrist works too, at a wrist's pace (a turn away from how the fingers pointed as they came together needn't be as quick as a dart of the whole hand, and a camera slowed to 15 frames a second in dim light reads it as well): with two fingers up it scrolls the way they flicked, as far as their tips swept, the page gliding on as a trackpad's does once a flicking finger lifts (even if the hand stops dead with its fingers still together, or the camera loses them mid-flick); with three it swipes, left or right between Spaces. Bringing the hand back doesn't scroll or swipe back, and flicking again the same way goes on. To go the other way, hold it still a moment first; with two fingers, that also stops the glide, and moving slowly scrolls as before. Curling the fingers in to let go isn't a flick. Both hands pinching zoom the app in front: draw them apart to zoom in, together to zoom out. It says what it's doing in signs rather than words: Gemini's ♊︎ while scrolling, a triangle while swiping, ⊕ and ⊖ as it zooms, a padlock if it can't click. To put it down, make the sign again, or rest the pointer a moment on the little sundial in the top bar, left of the band's page. It needs Accessibility, for the clicks, scrolls, swipes and zooms; without it the pointer moves but can't click. Gestures have to be on.

## Building

Needs macOS 14.2 or later and the Xcode Command Line Tools. No Xcode project.

```sh
./build.sh          # builds build/Oyl.app
./build.sh --run    # builds, then (re)launches it
```

The first build makes a local signing certificate in its own keychain, so macOS keeps the app's permissions across rebuilds.

The app icon (just Oyl, on a dark tile) is drawn with the app's own Oyl: `Icon/make.sh` redraws it from `Icon/Icon.swift` into `Icon/Oyl.icns`, which the build puts in the app.

## Claude Code

Oyl follows Claude Code sessions through hooks, which it installs as scripts in `~/Library/Application Support/nott/` each time it starts. Point Claude Code's hooks at `agent-hook` for its events, and at `permission-hook` for `PermissionRequest` to answer approvals from Oyl. A request for your OK, Claude Code's or the Claude app's, comes up as a small card in the screen's bottom right corner rather than opening the notch: who's asking, what it wants (and the command, file or site), Deny and Allow (or a tap of the right Command key on its own, just that once, a quarter of a second after in case a second tap's coming, since two are a screenshot; or a thumbs up while the camera's looking), and a click on the rest to answer it in the app instead; with more waiting, how many. In Oyl's look it's a black card with the asking Oyl on it; in Midnight's, a patch of night with no edge, a star glinting for the agent among a few twinkling specks, coming up out of a blur. It's there on every Space, over fullscreen apps too. A session's Oyl goes when it ends or its process quits, and once it's been idle ten minutes (finished in an editor's panel or a terminal's tab left open), coming back as soon as it's at work again; so does every agent's but Cowork's tasks, which go a quarter of an hour after they last did anything. Stopped with Esc, Claude Code doesn't say so through its hooks, so Oyl reads it off the end of the session's transcript and lets its Oyl rest.

The calendar bar reads the Mac's calendars, in each calendar's own color, so an account's calendars show up once it's added in System Settings › Internet Accounts. Clicking the bar opens Calendar.
