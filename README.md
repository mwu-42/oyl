# Oyl

**Own your life.** That's what Oyl stands for: a macOS notch app, and Oyl, the little imp who lives in it, keeping an eye on everything going on for you (your agents at work, your music, your timers, your Mac) so you don't have to.

Hover the notch for music (with a visualizer Oyl surfs across as the song plays, which you can drag Oyl along to scrub, lyrics that stay on from song to song until you turn them off (synced lyrics from LRCLIB, a free database people add songs to, so one just out may not have them for a while: a song it hasn't is asked after again an hour on, a lookup that got no answer the next time the song plays, and a title with a featured artist or a version on it, "(feat. …)" or " - Remastered", is looked for without that too; in Midnight they float in the sky under the open notch, whatever page is up, the sung line's words lighting like stars as the last line dissolves into stardust; the phone remote shows them too; and Lyrics on the bar, a setting in either look, puts them on the menu bar too: in Oyl's look the music Oyl sings each line in a bubble as it's sung (with it off, the Oyl DJs instead, whatever the lyrics setting), and in Midnight they stay up while the notch is closed, right in the menu bar's strip beside it, on no ground of their own: each line fades in faintly, then sparkles in word by word on the other side of the notch from the last (a break between them aside, so they're sung either side about as much), the first on the left, as the last fades slowly away, a long one stretching most of the way along its side before it shrinks; a break in a song, and a song without lyrics all through, is a constellation either side of the notch, flaring and rippling to the beat; from song to song, and as the music pauses and plays, they fade quickly out and in; and never in the way of a click), cover-art controls (always the song's own cover: Spotify shows a song switched to its music video with a still from the video, so Oyl looks up the song's cover instead, the video playing on in Spotify), and the song's name, which a click takes you to Spotify or Music from, with everyone it's by under it (Spotify's scripting tells only a song's first artist, so Oyl reads them all off the song's embed page on Spotify's site as it starts, sending it only the song's ID; they ease in a moment after, and the music Oyl's row and its hover say them too); a song Spotify's playing on another device, like your phone, is followed as the Mac's Spotify tells of each change (a song starting, a pause, a skip, a seek), so its lyrics keep time, though its scripting can go on saying an old song's playing here; with nothing told yet, a song that isn't heard on the Mac counts as paused), timers and reminders (a name can be as long as you like, wrapping onto as many lines as it takes, and ⌘C, ⌘V, ⌘X, ⌘A and ⌘Z work as they do anywhere; click one to change its time or name, or + to type how much more it gets, which lengthens what it was set for as well as what's left; one that's gone off can have one or five more; pin one to hold it on the closed notch, its ring and name left of the notch (an unnamed one, just its ring) and its time right, in either look, and from the phone too; or type “/pin” anywhere on its line, as you start it (“25m essay /pin”) or change it, or in its name on the phone), an Amphetamine-style keep-awake (type “+30m” for more of a set time; with the lid closed and no other screen on, once it's stayed down two seconds, it turns the screen and the keyboard's light all the way down and off, putting them back as they were last set while you were using the Mac, not as auto-brightness dims them as the lid comes down, mutes the Mac's own speakers (not headphones; Mute speakers when shut) and, if you like, puts on Low Power Mode (Low Power Mode when shut, which asks for your password once; on battery, then and below 40% or while it's running hot), and Oyl stops drawing, analyzing the music and polling the player till the lid opens, the screen woken as it does; it stops by itself on battery at 20% or if the battery can't be read twice running, and always if the Mac gets critically hot, saying why; its hold on idle sleep lets go by itself at a set time's end, even if Oyl's hung; and if Oyl crashes or is force-quit, a watchdog it starts alongside turns lid sleep back on (and Low Power Mode off) a minute later unless it's back, as it does if Oyl's hung over two minutes past a set time's end. Much of it after Insomnia's way of doing it), the Oyls page (where timers that went off in the past day rest, a click from running again), an activity monitor that also counts Claude Code tokens, and what they'd cost at Anthropic's API prices (hour by hour, today and in the last five hours: each reply priced by its model, cache reads and writes, fast mode and web searches included, from the pricing page as of November 2026; a subscription doesn't charge by the token, so it's what the same use would cost on the API), and under them your GitHub contributions over the past year, the grid on your profile (a square a day, a week to a column, brighter the more you did; commits, pull requests, issues and reviews as GitHub counts them, private ones too), read with GitHub's own gh tool as it's signed in (so Oyl keeps no sign-in of its own; without it, or signed out, the grid says how to set it up), when the page opens and every ten minutes while it's open, the last one kept so it's there at once. Beside it, the year's total; point at a day for its count and date, click it to see what you did that day on GitHub. In Oyl's look it's GitHub's grid in the page's pink, today ringed; in Midnight Oyl's a star chart, each day a star from a mote of dust to a four-pointed star for the busiest, the bright ones twinkling and a run of them in a week joined into a constellation, today's circled, the one pointed at swelling in moonlight. GitHub grid in the settings turns it off. Then a mirror (the camera, for a look at yourself before a call; its tab can be turned off on the Oyls tab). Out on the menu bar, an Oyl turns up for each thing going on (and when it's over, waves goodbye where it stands, springs up twice and is gone in a puff of smoke, sparks of its color flying out of it; one whose work is done jumps for joy first; in Midnight a moyl bows, bobs twice and flares, scattering into stardust. No two ever walk along together looking like one: one sets off a moment after another, a little quicker or slower, or somewhere else): each Claude Code agent at work (in a pastel with an orange hat; in a terminal, an editor or the Claude app's Code tab), each of the Claude app's Cowork tasks while it works and for a quarter of an hour after (read off the transcript each keeps on the Mac, whether or not it's the one on screen, in any of the app's accounts), the chat showing in the Claude app, Muse (Meta's agent, open at muse.ai in Chrome or Safari, in a black hat: working while its page offers to stop and what it says is changing (Muse leaves its Stop up once it's done, so a page that's sat still a minute is done), and wanting you while it asks you to approve something, then gone once it's answered; a page that's offered to stop for over five minutes is at a task of its own in the background, so doesn't count; read while its tab's the one showing, which needs Oyl allowed to control the browser and in Accessibility; click it to bring its tab up), every other agent on the radar (below), an app taking notes (Granola in a meeting: a sage Oyl with a notepad, scribbling, now and then looking up at what's being said, for as long as it has the microphone, which macOS tells of any app without asking; in Midnight, a banded world ruled like paper, a little moon going busily round it for the pen), the song (in headphones, dragging its cover and a little visualizer along behind it, bopping its head to the beat, which Oyl finds in the music as it plays, and singing along once a pinky's shown the lyrics, if they're on the bar (below); for an audiobook, sat reading it instead, looking up now and then to think it over, its cover in a thought cloud), every timer and reminder (its countdown beside it, or with Countdowns off on the Oyls tab, only in its last three minutes), staying awake, a low battery, a late night, a fresh selfie. At the top left of the menu bar is the name of the desktop you're on: “desktop 2”, as Mission Control numbers them, or, in a fullscreen app's Space, the app's name, in lower case, till you call it something else (kept just as you type it, capitals and all). It's up for 3 seconds each time you go to a desktop (and as Oyl or it is turned on), then fades: longer while the pointer's on it, or while you're renaming it, and for 3 seconds more once you have. Click it while it's up to rename it (Return keeps what's typed, Escape doesn't, clicking anywhere else keeps it too; nothing typed puts its own name back); a fullscreen app's name stays with its window, even as it leaves fullscreen and comes back, till the Mac restarts. In Oyl's look it's a piece of the notch in black, flush with the screen's top and left, its corner rounded and its top flared into the edge, a new desktop's name fading in over the last; in Midnight's, a patch of night with no edge, a glint twinkling before the name that flares as you go to another desktop, the name coming in out of a blur. On a desktop it sits over the Apple menu (and the app's, as far as it reaches), so a right-click on it opens whichever of them is under it (that needs Accessibility); in a fullscreen app it's in the black strip by the notch, and the menu bar slides down over it as ever, a click on it then going to the menu bar. The Oyls keep off it, and it's away while Oyl's off or resting, in Mission Control and under Midnight's band; Desktop's name on the Oyls tab turns it off.

Picture in picture works for any window, and over every desktop and every fullscreen app (a browser's own doesn't come over another app's fullscreen Space): ⌃⌥P puts the window in front into it, small (the one the app has its focus in, as Accessibility says; never the strip a fullscreen app keeps its toolbar in, as Chrome does), and again puts it away (or puts the new window in front in its place); or right-click an open app in Oyl's Dock for Picture in Picture (a menu of its windows, if it has a few; Close Picture in Picture, if one of them's in it). One at a time. It's a live copy of the window, wherever that is: on another desktop, in its own fullscreen Space, under other windows. It's drawn by ScreenCaptureKit, so Oyl has to be allowed Screen Recording (till it is, it says so, and a click on it opens the setting), only at as many pixels as it's shown at (more as you zoom in), thirty frames a second, only while it's up; macOS's screen-recording mark shows in the menu bar meanwhile. It takes the window's shape from what ScreenCaptureKit sends, and shows just the window in it, wherever in the frame it's put. Drag it anywhere; drag a corner to make it bigger or smaller, or an edge to change its shape (the window then fills it, what's over cropped off). Pinch, or ⌘-scroll, to zoom in on part of the window (a video on a page, say), scroll to move about it, a two-finger double tap zooms in or back out, and Fit puts it back as it was. Double-click it, or its arrow, to go to the window (its app comes forward and macOS goes to the Space it's on); × puts it away. The next one comes up where and as big as the last was (low on the right, the first time), and it goes once the window's closed or its app quits, Oyl's switched off, or the feature is; it's out of the way in Mission Control. In Oyl's look it's the window with rounded corners and a shadow, × and the arrow coming up either end of a shade along its top, the window's name between, on a plain ease, as the pointer comes over it. In Midnight Oyl's it has no edge: the window fades out into the desktop all round, a glint twinkling at its top right and a fainter one low on its left, motes of starlight drifting round in the fading edge; × is traced in silver at its top left and the arrow at its bottom right, the app's name in small capitals along the bottom, and it comes in out of a blur and goes back into one. ⌃⌥P Picture in picture on the Keys tab turns it off.

Four fingers up (or the right Shift key twice, quickly) shows your desktops in a list: your desktops and fullscreen apps, in the order they're in, each by its name (what you've called it, or "desktop 2", or a fullscreen app's own), with the icons of the apps that have windows on it beside it, and a desktop still called by its number naming them too. It comes up with your fingers and settles as they lift: most of the way (or a flick) and it's up, otherwise it goes back down. Click one and the list is gone at once and you're there, with no slide: each step is a swipe posted to the Dock so fast it's over before it's begun (as InstantSpaceSwitcher does on macOS 26), and if that hasn't got there a moment later, Mission Control's move-a-space shortcuts take it the rest of the way; right-click one to rename it (Return keeps the name, Escape doesn't, and nothing puts its own name back), the same names the menu bar's label shows. ↑ and ↓ pick a row, and Return goes to it. Drag one desktop onto another and they're put together in a group, named "group" (or "group 2", and so on) and ready to be renamed, its heading where the first of them comes in the list and its desktops in under it; drag another onto the heading (or onto a desktop in it) to put it in, or off the list to take it out; or drag one between two lines, as if moving it there (a line shows where): outside its group's run, it's taken out; between another group's desktops, it's put in that group; and while one's dragged, what letting go would do is said under the list (before there are any groups, how to make one is). Or shift-click one, then shift-click another: everything between them (a folded group's desktops too) is made a group, lit as you point before you click, the count said under the list; shift-clicking the same one again, or Escape, takes the mark off. Right-click a heading to rename it. Click a heading to fold its group away under it (folded, it says how many are in it and shows their apps' icons, and the one you're on, folded away, marks it), or ← and → with it picked; point at it to ungroup it, and at a desktop in a group to take it out. A fullscreen app keeps its place in a group when it leaves fullscreen and comes back. Put a group's desktops together into one fullscreen Space (two apps side by side, in Mission Control or by tiling a window into another's) and once it's taken them all, the group's done away with, a group of one being none; with any of its desktops left out, it stays. Your groups show over macOS's own Mission Control too (Groups in Mission Control, in the Keys section): while it's open, Oyl looks at its row of desktops through accessibility (only looks: nothing's pressed or dragged, and every click goes through) and marks each group's run of them, in Oyl's look a white bar under them and the group's name on a black pill, in Midnight Oyl's a fine silver line with a star under each desktop and the name in italics on a breath of night, following the row as it opens out under the pointer and as desktops are dragged about. A group whose desktops aren't next to each other there is marked dashed, in amber (in Midnight, in pale amber), its parts linked by a curve under the row: only the groups' names are words. Oyl's own list marks the group's heading with the same amber dashes. Oyl looks for the row as soon as three fingers begin up (and when the Dock says Mission Control's open), keeps the marks while it's there, and puts them away the moment Mission Control goes (as you go to a desktop or come back to yours). The groups are only the list's: the list can't put the desktops in another order, as macOS keeps that in the Dock, and short of turning System Integrity Protection off, only dragging them in Mission Control changes it. Four fingers down (or three), right Shift twice again, Escape, or a click off the list puts it away (⇧⇧ Desktops, in the Keys section, turns the key off), three fingers down following your fingers just as four do. While it's up, three fingers up don't open Mission Control over it, and three down put it away rather than opening App Exposé; a swipe across still goes to the next desktop, and puts the list away as it begins (so it's never left up on the desktop you've gone from, out of sight, holding Mission Control back); going to another desktop any other way with it up puts it away too, unless it's only just come up (four fingers up as the last swipe across was still sliding), when it's brought along to the desktop you've come to rather than left on the one slid away from. And should Mission Control open or start to (a swipe taken for its as the fingers come down) with the list up, the list's brought to the front again as it goes, never left out of sight. macOS can leave a window that's to be on every Space on just some of them (the list, brought up while Mission Control was open, has been seen left on just the first desktop and one fullscreen app's), and nothing an app does with the window wins the rest back; so as the list comes up it's put back on every Space it's missing (by the window server's own call for it, which it allows on an app's own windows), and so are all of Oyl's every-Space windows (Mission Control's group marks, the lyrics, the bar's limit) as they come up and whenever the Space changes. Should the list still not be on the desktop you're on, it doesn't come up at all, and while it's up it's looked for twice a second: out of sight, it's brought back, or else put away, so it's never up unseen holding three fingers back. Over it is the time and the day, since a fullscreen app keeps the menu bar's clock out of sight (on the Mac's own 12- or 24-hour clock; Time in the desktops, in the Keys section, takes it off). In Oyl's look it's a plain black card a little way down the middle of the screen, the one you're on in bold with a dot, a group's heading a small bold name after a chevron that turns as it folds. The time's large over the card, the day under it. In Midnight Oyl's the screen goes to night, stars twinkling over ink, and the list is set off to the left under "the desktops" in a flourish, the time over that in Superclarendon, its colon twinkling like a star, and the day in italic as it would be written ("tuesday, the 25th of november"), drifting in out of a blur, the names in Superclarendon strung down a line of stars that bows out and back, the one you're on lit by a twinkling glint, each row drifting in out of a blur after the one before; a group's heading is a little constellation and its name in italics, its desktops further along the line, drifting away to the left into a blur as it folds (the constellation drawing in close). Oyl reads the trackpad's fingers itself (through Apple's private multitouch framework, as BetterTouchTool does), so macOS's own four-finger swipe has to be off: while Mission Control's swipe is on, macOS takes four fingers up too (setting it to three fingers turns four on as well). With Mission Control on three fingers, Free four fingers (in the Keys section of the settings, under Four fingers up: desktops) turns macOS's four-finger swipe up and down off and leaves Mission Control its three; otherwise set Mission Control to three fingers or off in System Settings › Trackpad › More Gestures. Till then, four fingers up are left to Mission Control.

Oyl watches for agents universally, on any Mac, with its agent radar: a list of the agents it knows and how each gives itself away, since there's no one sign on a Mac that an AI is at work. Claude Code by its hooks (or, where they aren't set up, as a program); the Claude app's chats by its window (even from another Space: in a fullscreen one of its own, its window's still read, and one asking for your OK to use a tool shows as waiting on you; so does a task waiting on you that isn't the one showing, as its sidebar says ("Awaiting …", "Needs …")), and Cowork by the transcripts its tasks keep. Agents and chats in a browser tab by their pages, which nearly all offer to stop while they work and ask you to approve or allow what they want to do: Muse, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, Grok, Copilot, DeepSeek, Le Chat, Manus, Lovable, v0, Bolt and Replit. Apps by their windows, the same way: the ChatGPT app, Codex and Perplexity. Programs run in a terminal or an editor by how busy they are (one at its prompt uses next to nothing; one at work, streaming its reply, a steady few percent of a core), each its own Oyl named for the folder it's working in, while it's at work and a while after: Codex, Gemini CLI, Aider, OpenCode, Goose, Amp, Cursor Agent, Crush, Qwen Code and Copilot CLI. And note takers by the microphone: Granola. Each agent's Oyl wears its own color of hat while it works (Claude's orange, ChatGPT's and Codex's green, Gemini's blue, Muse's black…). For anything else, bring up the app (or the site, in a browser) and right-click the notch: Watch it as an Agent, and it's on the list, watched by its windows (or its page) from then on; Stop Watching takes it off. And Oyl looks for agents it doesn't know: every half minute, which apps and programs hold a connection open to an AI service (OpenAI's, Anthropic's, Mistral's, Groq's, OpenRouter's, Deepgram's and the like), and one that keeps doing so is offered, once, at the foot of the Oyls page: "Notion seems to use AI. Watch it as an agent?" (No, and it's never asked again.) Browsers aren't, since a browser is one app for all its tabs, nor editors built on VS Code, whose windows can't be read without switching on their screen-reader support. All of it is read on the Mac, from what macOS tells any app of its own user's processes; nothing is sent anywhere.

Claude's limits run in 5-hour windows, each starting with your first prompt. ☀ Claude at 7:00 AM, in the Agents section of the settings (off till you turn it on, the time moved a quarter of an hour at a time with ‹ and ›), starts yours early: each morning at that time Oyl sends Claude a one-word prompt through Claude Code's own command, on your plan, answered by Haiku so it takes as little of the limit as anything can, and the window resets five hours on, that much sooner in the day. It runs in a folder of Oyl's own with no tools, no MCP servers, no hooks and nothing saved, so it's no session anywhere and no agent Oyl. The Mac has to be awake for it: missed while it sleeps, it's sent when it wakes, if that's within four hours, and tried again a few minutes on if it fails (a few times at most). Its hint says when it last went, or why it didn't. A new window doesn't start by itself when one ends, only with your next prompt; ↻ And as each one ends (just under it, off to begin with) keeps them going one after another: half a minute after a window ends, Oyl reads /usage, and if none's going (you've not been using Claude meanwhile), the same one-word prompt starts the next; if one is, it waits for that one to end. Only from the morning's time till 2 AM, so from 8:00 that's 8, 1, 6 and 11 (and from 7:00, 7, 12, 5 and 10), and never two less than four and a half hours apart. How much of the window is used shows on the usage page (the CPU tab), in the page's pink (everything on it is), under Claude's tokens (Claude's 5 hours, in the Notch section of the settings): as Claude Code's own /usage says, read every few minutes (and as the page opens, soon after a Claude that was working is done, when the Mac wakes, and as the window resets), which Claude Code answers itself without a prompt; in Oyl's look a bar filling, in Midnight Oyl's a moon waxing from a crescent to full, with the figure and when it resets. Commits, under them (in the Notch section of the settings, or on the row's right-click), is a timeline of your commits as Claude's tokens are: today's, hour by hour, how many today and in the last five hours, and the repository with the most, from the git repositories Claude Code's worked in (remembered once seen, so one worked on by hand afterwards still counts), yours only (by the repository's own email), on its local branches, unpushed ones too, read once a minute while the page is up, git only read. 5 hours on the bar (in the Notch section too, off to begin with) shows what's left of it on the menu bar just right of the notch, "5h 81% left": in Oyl's look a small piece of the notch in black, flared into the top, the figure in white, amber under 20% left and coral under 5%; in Midnight Oyl's a patch of night with no edge and a moon that wanes as the window's used. It never takes the pointer, and it's away while the notch is open, in Mission Control, under Midnight's band, and when there's no window going; the lyrics on the bar keep clear of it. 🔔 Sound when Claude's done (in the Agents section, off to begin with) plays a sound as a Claude finishes its turn and another as it starts waiting for you, for a permission or a question: Oyl's own tones, low and round rather than a bright bling, three soft notes rising for done, a small ta-da, and two soft taps of one for waiting (in Midnight Oyl's softer still, slower to fade, with a faint shimmer over them), never more than one a second and a half, and none for a Claude you're looking at already (its app in front, and in an editor, its folder's window). Each place a Claude runs has its own row under it once one's been seen there (VS Code, Terminal, the Claude app, Cowork…), and Everywhere else for the rest: click a row to hear it, ‹ › for another, from Oyl's eight named tones (Ember, the usual, Hearth, Driftwood, Lantern, Moonrise, Pebble, Low Tide and Ripple, each low and round and each its own tune, done rising and waiting asking), any sound file put in Oyl's sounds folder (~/Library/Application Support/nott/sounds, opened by Sounds folder…), macOS's own, or none; a place with none of its own sounds as everywhere else does. Each desktop can have its own too, from the desktops' list (four fingers up): point at one and click its sound button ("sound", in Midnight) for a list of the sounds beside it, as a phone lists its alarm sounds, and click one to hear it and pick it (its app's sound, none of its own, is first); a desktop with its own says it on its row (♪ Moonrise). A Claude's desktop is the one its app's windows are all on, or else the one with the window whose title names its folder; its desktop's sound plays if it has one, else its app's. And the desktop a sound came from is marked in the list for a quarter of an hour, or till you go to it: in Oyl's look its row tinted amber with a bar at its edge and a bell for one waiting for you, or mint with a check for one that's done; in Midnight Oyl's a wash of light across it and the same marks by its name, a check traced in starlight or a pale amber bell, a star twinkling at the tip (so swapping the look never loses what's marked: the marks, the groups and the names are the list's, not the look's); the newest breathes, slowly brighter and back, to catch the eye; a folded group shows its desktops' latest on its heading. A desktop with agents at work on it says so too, by its name: in Oyl's look a breathing dot in Claude's orange, with how many if more than one; in Midnight Oyl's a little pale gold moon going round, the count in italic (a folded group, its desktops' all told). Only one actually at it counts, thinking, writing or running something, not one waiting on you, done or idle; and since Claude Code says when it starts on something but not always when it stops, one that's said nothing, and written nothing in its transcript, for 45 seconds is looked at there: one whose turn's over (its reply ending the turn, stopped with Esc, or one of Claude Code's own commands like /model) isn't at work, while one at a tool or waiting on its reply still is (by its transcript rather than how busy its process is, since thinking at length it can be still for minutes). The marks keep up as agents start and stop while the list's up. Which desktop is read off the Claude's app's windows; where they can't tell (an editor with a window on each of several desktops, whose titles macOS keeps from Oyl without Screen Recording), it's the desktop you were on when you last looked at that Claude (its app in front, and in an editor its folder's window), or where its windows last told, which Oyl notes as you go (with the sounds or the list on) and keeps across its relaunches, for a fortnight.

Every feature can be turned off. The settings tab, the gear last in the notch's row of tabs (or Settings… on the notch's right-click), is a page of them all, in sections: the notch's pages (each tab can go), lyrics, the visualizer (and with it, listening to the music), the song's name opening its player, haptics, Wispr Flow; each kind of Oyl (agents', the music's, timers', keep-awake's, the battery's, bedtime's, the selfie's, the one hanging out), the countdowns, dimming the bar; each agent, the radar's and yours (yours with an × to take them off the list), answering Claude Code in the notch, and looking for new ones; hand gestures, and each gesture on its own; the control taps, the song keys and keeping out of Mission Control's way; the Dock, each shelf, and labeling screenshots; Midnight Oyl, and opening at login. Point at one to see what it does. In Midnight Oyl the page is engraved, its rows drifting in one after another; under them all, the motto: Oyl, own your life. Many are a right-click away where they're about, too: right-click a page for its switches (the music page for the lyrics, on the bar or under the song, the visualizer and the music Oyl; the timers page for the timer Oyls and their countdowns; the Oyls page for which agents are watched, their Oyls, the sound when Claude's done and the morning start; the stats page for its rows), right-click the 5-hour row on the stats page to show what's left on the bar beside the notch, to start the window each morning or to hide the row, right-click GitHub's grid to hide it, and right-click a tab to hide its page; each menu ends with Settings…, opened on the section the rest are in.

The shelf, Recent, in Oyl's Dock (below), keeps what's new in one row, newest first, each tile marked with a dot for where it came from: your latest screenshots and screen recordings as you take them, wherever screenshots are set to go (light green); whatever you've just saved or downloaded (blue, below); and the files Claude Code's just written or edited (orange: Oyl hears of each Write, Edit and notebook edit through its hooks once it's done, not Claude's own files in ~/.claude, what it only scribbles in a temporary folder, or a repository's .git; the latest eight still there, remembered across launches, a file it made in Downloads showing as Claude's). Pinching your index finger and thumb clicks (holding the pinch holds, to drag; two quick pinches double-click), and so does resting the pointer on something a moment, a ring of dots drawing round it till it clicks. Touching your thumb to your middle fingertip instead, the index still pointing, right-clicks. Two fingers held together, side by side, are two on the trackpad: move them to scroll. Three together swipe as the trackpad's set to: across between Spaces and full-screen apps, up for Mission Control, down for the app's windows. A flick of the wrist works too, at a wrist's pace (a turn away from how the fingers pointed as they came together needn't be as quick as a dart of the whole hand, and a camera slowed to 15 frames a second in dim light reads it as well): with two fingers up it scrolls the way they flicked, as far as their tips swept, the page gliding on as a trackpad's does once a flicking finger lifts (even if the hand stops dead with its fingers still together, or the camera loses them mid-flick); with three it swipes, left or right between Spaces. Bringing the hand back doesn't scroll or swipe back, and flicking again the same way goes on. To go the other way, hold it still a moment first; with two fingers, that also stops the glide, and moving slowly scrolls as before. Curling the fingers in to let go isn't a flick. Both hands pinching zoom the app in front: draw them apart to zoom in, together to zoom out. It says what it's doing in signs rather than words: Gemini's ♊︎ while scrolling, a triangle while swiping, ⊕ and ⊖ as it zooms, a padlock if it can't click. To put it down, make the sign again, or rest the pointer a moment on the little sundial in the top bar, left of the band's page. It needs Accessibility, for the clicks, scrolls, swipes and zooms; without it the pointer moves but can't click. Gestures have to be on.

toyl (things off your list, ~/toyl), tod's successor, is a Mac app of its own, and once it's installed (its list made, in ~/Library/Application Support/toyl, or wherever TOYL_HOME says) Oyl works on toyl's list instead of tod's, and lets toyl stand in for its own to-do pieces; toyl on the settings tab (only there with toyl installed) turns that off, and Oyl goes back to tod and its own, as it does if toyl's list goes away. With it on, the ideas page is toyl's ideas: its ideas list, and anything put off for someday in its other lists (not those it's archived); what's jotted there goes in through toyl's own command, so it's read just as toyl reads it, and since toyl has no aims, "by fri" or "next month" is a deadline (an idea with only an aim from before shows as someday), so there's nothing to put off a week or a month, and one pulled in from an email carries a small envelope, an assignment toyl's brought in from Canvas a mortarboard. The inbox page adds through toyl's own quick add, and the phone remote's ideas and everything Oyl saves work on toyl's list under the same lock on its folder, every field Oyl doesn't use kept as it was, so nothing either saves is lost; a #name no list has makes a toyl list with one section, not tod's three lanes, and each task Oyl adds or changes is stamped as toyl stamps its own. toyl's timers come too, and it's the one timer system: a countdown started in toyl (on a task, on its timers page, or by an agent through its command line or MCP server) shows here as Oyl's own do, held on the closed notch if toyl asks (a focus timer does), and rings here, toyl leaving the ringing to Oyl; paused, given more time or stopped here, it's paused, lengthened or stopped in toyl too, and the time it ran goes on its task, and into toyl's log. One started here, on the timers page, from the phone, by running one that went off again or by snoozing one, starts in toyl's file (on the task of the same name, if there is one), and with no time said it runs toyl's half hour. toyl's stopwatches stay in toyl. toyl's tasks don't ring on the notch: its timers do. The projects outline (its window, its reminders and Projects… on the notch's right-click) is gone meanwhile, since toyl's lists and groups cover it (it's kept, for when toyl's off). toyl's phone page is its own; the phone remote keeps to what's Oyl's.

## Building

Needs macOS 14.2 or later and the Xcode Command Line Tools. No Xcode project.

```sh
./build.sh          # builds build/Oyl.app
./build.sh --run    # builds, then (re)launches it
```

The first build makes a local signing certificate in its own keychain, so macOS keeps the app's permissions across rebuilds.

The app icon (just Oyl, on a dark tile) is drawn with the app's own Oyl: `Icon/make.sh` redraws it from `Icon/Icon.swift` into `Icon/Oyl.icns`, which the build puts in the app.

## Claude Code

Oyl follows Claude Code sessions through hooks, which it installs as scripts in `~/Library/Application Support/nott/` each time it starts. Point Claude Code's hooks at `agent-hook` for its events. Requests for your OK aren't answered from Oyl: Claude Code asks for them where it runs, as ever, and an agent waiting on you shows as waiting on its Oyl. (`permission-hook`, from when they were, is still written so an old `PermissionRequest` entry in Claude Code's settings points at something, but it says nothing and returns at once; that entry can go.) A session's Oyl goes when it ends or its process quits, and once it's been idle ten minutes (finished in an editor's panel or a terminal's tab left open), coming back as soon as it's at work again; so does every agent's but Cowork's tasks, which go a quarter of an hour after they last did anything. Stopped with Esc, Claude Code doesn't say so through its hooks, so Oyl reads it off the end of the session's transcript and lets its Oyl rest.
